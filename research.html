Research

JWST has revealed a population of faint, broad-line sources in the early universe that was invisible to every previous survey. My work asks what these objects are, how many of them there are, and what they imply for the growth of the first supermassive black holes.

Broad Hα emitters in GOODS-N

Using the complete NIRCam grism coverage of GOODS-N from CONGRESS and FRESCO, together with JADES imaging, I assembled a large sample of broad Hα emitters at z ≈ 4–6. Slitless spectroscopy over a full field gives an essentially unbiased census: every source with a broad line in the wavelength range is recovered, without pre-selection on color or morphology.

The resulting number densities are far higher than pre-JWST expectations for faint AGN, which pushes on how early black holes assemble their mass and how much of that growth is obscured.

Little red dots

Little red dots are compact, extremely red, often broad-lined sources that appear in nearly every deep JWST field. Whether they are dust-reddened AGN, unusually dense stellar systems, or something in between is still open. I contribute to work on their selection and colors, their resolved UV morphology, and their host properties — the questions that decide which interpretation survives.

Galaxy evolution and star formation histories

Earlier in my career I developed a spectroscopic diagnostic to identify rejuvenating galaxies — systems that quenched and then restarted star formation — from the combination of features sensitive to young and old stellar populations. I remain interested in how AGN-driven multiphase outflows shape the star formation histories of their hosts.
